The science of NAD+

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every living cell. It plays two essential roles: it helps your mitochondria convert nutrients into ATP — the energy currency of the body — and it activates repair enzymes (sirtuins and PARPs) involved in healthy aging.

Why levels matter

Research links declining NAD+ to many of the hallmarks of aging. Levels drop steadily with age — by some estimates roughly halving between young adulthood and midlife — alongside factors like stress, poor sleep, and metabolic load. Replenishing NAD+ is one of the most studied levers in the longevity field.

Why transdermal delivery

Oral NAD+ faces a hurdle called first-pass metabolism: much of it is degraded in the digestive tract and liver before reaching circulation. Transdermal delivery bypasses the gut, releasing the compound gradually through the skin for steadier, more sustained exposure — without the spike-and-crash of a pill or the cost and clinical setting of an IV.
